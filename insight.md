# **Insights & Analytical Findings**

* **JobLevel Drives Income, Not Department:** 
  Monthly income is primarily dictated by **JobLevel** rather than departmental placement. Median income scales predictably from Level 1 (~$2,400–$2,650) up to Level 5 (~$18,000–$18,800) across all departments, proving that career progression is the dominant compensation multiplier.

* **Departmental Baseline Comparison:** 
  **Sales** holds the highest overall median monthly income (~$5,779), followed by **Research & Development** (~$4,242) and **Human Resources** (~$3,059). This reflects variance in entry-level headcount proportions across divisions.

* **Job Satisfaction Vulnerabilities:** 
  While overall satisfaction skews positive (levels 3 and 4), **Human Resources** exhibits the highest relative dissatisfaction rate (~27.27% at Level 1 satisfaction), highlighting a specific retention risk area compared to Sales and R&D.

* **Work-Life Balance Benchmark:** 
  Over **61%** of the total workforce rates their work-life balance as "Better" (Level 3), with **23%** rating it "Good" (Level 2). Only **6%** report a "Bad" (Level 1) work-life balance, establishing a solid organizational benchmark.
